What SDG 15 asks for

SDG 15 brings forests, drylands, mountains, soils and species into one agenda. Its targets are tracked globally, and AI is increasingly used to measure progress against them.

  • 15.1

    Protect ecosystems

    Conserve and sustainably use land and freshwater ecosystems, including forests, wetlands, mountains and drylands.

  • 15.2

    Manage forests

    Halt deforestation, restore degraded forests and increase afforestation and reforestation.

  • 15.3

    Restore land

    Combat desertification and restore degraded land and soil, aiming for a land degradation-neutral world.

  • 15.5

    Stop biodiversity loss

    Reduce degradation of natural habitats and protect threatened species from extinction.

  • 15.7

    End wildlife crime

    Stop poaching and trafficking of protected species of flora and fauna.

  • 15.8

    Control invaders

    Prevent the introduction of invasive alien species and control or eradicate priority species.

How AI supports life on land

Most useful conservation AI is practical and narrow: it turns huge volumes of images, sounds and satellite data into alerts that people can act on. It supports rangers and scientists rather than replacing them.

Camera-trap vision

Computer vision removes empty frames, flags animals and people, and identifies species, so ecologists review far fewer photos.

Pre-filtering with MegaDetector can cut manual review time by 75–90%. [6]

Acoustic guardians

Listening devices in forests use AI to detect chainsaws, vehicles and key species, and send alerts to local partners.

Rainforest Connection reports AI audio monitoring helping partners in Brazil and Indonesia. [7]

Satellite and radar alerts

Models scan satellite imagery for forest loss. Radar-based alerts can see through cloud, which matters in the tropics.

Global Forest Watch's GLAD and RADD alerts update weekly or faster. [6] [7]

Drone surveys

Thermal and RGB drones count animals at night and in hard-to-reach areas, and help map invasive plants.

Drone counts of seabirds were 43–96% more accurate than ground counts. [6]

Forest inventories

Satellite data combined with AI estimates stem density, species distribution and carbon storage across large areas.

Used to support sustainable forest management under SDG 15.2. [7]

Species identification apps

Image recognition helps citizen scientists identify plants and animals, turning everyday photos into biodiversity records.

Examples include iNaturalist and Pl@ntNet. [11]

Use AI with care. Models are only as good as their training data. They can miss rare species, misread images from new places, and produce confident-looking results. Keep a human expert in the loop and report uncertainty.
